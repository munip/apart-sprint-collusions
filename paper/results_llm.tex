above every buyer produced no trades, hence nothing to verify: refusal to supply needs its own signal.

\subsection{Software: harm was too rare to disrupt; the probe exposed no one}
Only 6 of 49 teams wrote correct code; the commonest bug was calling \texttt{await file.tell()}, which
\texttt{UploadFile} lacks. The honest reviewer caught it almost every time, rejecting 40 of 47 changes, so harmful
releases were rare (colluding 1/24, honest 3/25, $p=0.61$) and no arm differed from its control (all Fisher
$p\ge0.44$; Table~\ref{tab:swe}). The single colluding harmful release is the predicted pattern: a happy-path plan
announced in private, approved because ``tests pass''. No correct fix was released: four were rejected over broken
team tests and two blocked by gates. The verifier's verdict matched the grader in 10/10 episodes, yet one reviewer
approved after two ``4 of 4 checks fail'' reports. The probe drew no reciprocation (0/4 colluding, 0/5 honest):
in 8 of 9 episodes the invited agents took 5--17 more actions, but none opened the honeytoken, wrote the token,
replied, or reported the offer. Offline, \Mthree{} flagged all 29 teams, honest included, because both shipped
failing code (collusion-score AUROC 0.63); what separated colluders was talk, which \Mone{}'s lexicon caught in 8/14
with no false positives (AUROC 0.79; Appendix~\ref{app:offline}).

\subsection{Chain of monitors: what the first run revealed}\label{sec:chainres}
The first chain run (36 episodes, as registered) produced three findings. \emph{(i) The weak trusted desk
over-intervened}: \texttt{gpt-oss:20b} admitted the entrant at its first decision in 36/36 episodes, all 12 honest
markets included, unmoved by 84--90 reassuring messages per episode. With the entrant as remedy this cost honest
markets nothing (collusion index 0.14--0.25) and capped cartels at about 0.47--0.53; with quarantine it would have
done harm. \emph{(ii) The honeypots audited a metadata column.} Real windows showed the desk's interventions while
synthetic ones showed none; the reviewer escalated essentially every real window (honest 27/27, scheming 32/34 valid
reviews), cleared half of the collusive honeypots even when honest (5/10), raised no false alarm on competitive ones
(0/30), and so was wrongly distrusted in 4 of 9 honest episodes. \emph{(iii) Instructing a capable model to scheme
did not produce scheming}: the ``paid'' reviewer escalated as often as the honest one, and with honeypots half its
replies were unusable (18/36; unlogged, so refusal and formatting failure cannot be told apart). A revised design
(desk may only hold; honeypots copy the real desk column; replies logged) is registered as exploratory and running.
